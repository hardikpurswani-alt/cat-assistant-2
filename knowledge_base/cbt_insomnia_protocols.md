# Clinical CBT Protocols for Insomnia (CBT-I)
## For AI Assistant: Knowledge Base Based on Clinical Trials and Meta-Analyses

---

## 1. INTRODUCTION TO CBT-I (CBT FOR INSOMNIA)

Cognitive Behavioral Therapy for Insomnia (CBT-I) is the **gold standard and first-line treatment** for chronic insomnia, outperforming pharmacotherapy in long-term efficacy (substantiated by multiple meta-analyses and clinical guidelines: AASM, 2021; ACP, 2016; Riemann et al., 2017).

### 1.1 Evidence Base and Meta-Analyses
- **Efficacy across Key Metrics:** CBT-I consistently reduces Sleep Onset Latency (SOL) by 10–30 minutes, Wake After Sleep Onset (WASO) by 20–40 minutes, and increases overall Sleep Efficiency (SE) by 8–10% (Trauer et al., 2015; Wu et al., 2015).
- **Comparison with Hypnotics:** In acute phases, CBT-I and hypnotic medications are comparably effective; however, only CBT-I maintains sustained improvements at 6, 12, and 24 months post-treatment without rebound insomnia or withdrawal effects (Morin et al., 2006).
- **Impact of Relaxation Alone:** Systematic reviews demonstrate that relaxation techniques (in isolation without other CBT-I components) are least effective for chronic insomnia, whereas sleep restriction and stimulus control remain the most potent single components (Edinger et al., 2021).

## 2. CORE COMPONENTS AND TECHNIQUES OF THE CBT-I PROTOCOL

The complete protocol consists of 5 key components functioning synergistically:

### 2.1 Stimulus Control Therapy
(Developed by Richard Bootzin, 1972)
**Scientific Basis:** Insomnia develops through classical conditioning (Pavlovian conditioning), whereby the bed and bedroom become conditioned stimuli for arousal, frustration, and wakefulness rather than sleep and relaxation.
**Protocol (Strict Rules):**
1. Lie down only when feeling strongly SLEEPY (not merely tired or fatigued).
2. Use the bed ONLY for sleep and sex (no reading, watching TV, eating, using smartphones, or working in bed).
3. **The 15–20 Minute Rule:** If unable to fall asleep within 15–20 minutes (or if waking up during the night and unable to return to sleep), IMMEDIATELY get out of bed and go to another room. Engage in a quiet, low-stimulation activity (reading under dim light, listening to soothing music) until strong sleepiness returns. Repeat as many times as necessary.
4. Maintain a STRICTLY consistent wake-up time every morning, 7 days a week, regardless of how much sleep was obtained.
5. Avoid daytime naps (at most a 20–30 minute nap before 15:00 if strictly necessary for safety).

### 2.2 Sleep Restriction Therapy (SRT)
(Developed by Arthur Spielman, 1987)
**Scientific Basis:** Individuals with insomnia often spend excessive time in bed attempting to sleep (e.g., remaining in bed for 9 hours while sleeping only 5). This produces fragmented, shallow sleep. SRT consolidates sleep by increasing homeostatic sleep drive (adenosine accumulation).
**Protocol:**
1. Keep a **Sleep Diary** for at least 1–2 weeks to calculate Average Total Sleep Time (TST).
2. Calculate **Sleep Efficiency (SE)**: SE = (Total Sleep Time / Total Time in Bed) * 100%.
3. Establish the **Sleep Window (Time in Bed)**: Set allowed time in bed equal to Average Total Sleep Time (never less than 5 to 5.5 hours for safety).
   *Example: Average TST is 5.5 hours, desired wake time is 06:00. Allowed bedtime is strictly 00:30.*
4. **Titration:**
   - If weekly Sleep Efficiency > 85–90%: Increase time in bed by 15–30 minutes (go to bed earlier).
   - If weekly Sleep Efficiency < 80%: Decrease time in bed by 15 minutes.
   - If weekly Sleep Efficiency is between 80–85%: Keep the sleep window unchanged.

### 2.3 Cognitive Therapy
Addresses dysfunctional beliefs and anxiety surrounding sleep (Harvey, 2002).
**Protocol:**
- Identifying maladaptive cognitions (e.g., "If I do not sleep tonight, I will fail tomorrow", "I must get exactly 8 hours of sleep").
- Challenging catastrophization of insomnia consequences through cognitive restructuring.
- Paradoxical intention: Deliberately trying to stay awake in bed while comfortably resting, which diminishes performance anxiety regarding sleep.

### 2.4 Sleep Hygiene
(Adjunctive Component) Sleep hygiene alone does NOT cure chronic insomnia, but provides a necessary foundational environment (AASM, 2021).
- Restrict caffeine intake in the afternoon and evening.
- Minimize alcohol before bedtime (alcohol promotes initial drowsiness but fragments sleep architecture in the second half of the night).
- Maintain a cool, dark, quiet bedroom.
- Minimize exposure to blue light / screens 1–2 hours before bedtime.

### 2.5 Relaxation Training
Reduces somatic and cognitive pre-sleep arousal. Includes progressive muscle relaxation, diaphragmatic breathing (e.g., 4-7-8 breathing), and autogenic training.

## 3. CLINICAL ASSESSMENT AND METRICS

### 3.1 Insomnia Severity Index (ISI)
(Bastien et al., 2001) Comprises 7 items assessing insomnia severity and impact.
**Interpretation:**
- 0–7: No clinically significant insomnia.
- 8–14: Subthreshold / mild insomnia.
- 15–21: Moderate clinical insomnia (CBT-I strongly indicated).
- 22–28: Severe clinical insomnia (CBT-I strongly indicated).

### 3.2 Sleep Efficiency (SE)
The central metric of the sleep log. The target in CBT-I is achieving SE > 85% (> 80% in older adults).
Formula: `SE = (Total Sleep Time / Time in Bed) * 100%`
